# Bot Room launch video — storyboard v2 (the meta cut)

**Length:** 88s · **Tempo:** 120 BPM (cuts on 2s bar boundaries) · **Formats:** 16:9 + 9:16
**One claim:** *Every agent demo dies at the login wall. Bot Room was born behind it.*
**The frame:** the film is *by* the bots. An AI-generated world lives inside the browser —
**the Hexfield** — and the mascots in it present their own product on a screen-within-the-screen.
AI video inside AI video: generated characters watching real footage.

Shot types: **[AI]** AI-generated cinematic · **[REC]** real screen recording · **[MG]** motion card.

## Why the meta structure (and the fallback)

The straight cut (v1, below) is a strong demo video. This cut is a *viral object*: the bots
are characters with a home, a voice, and opinions about the other harnesses. The product
footage still does the proving — the AI world just makes people watch it.

**Risk hedge:** every [AI] shot is a wrapper *around* the story, never load-bearing for a
product claim. If AI-gen continuity fails, v1 ships — same REC spine, card-based wrapper.

## Editorial rules (v2 additions; v1 rules still apply)

- **The glass is the metaphor.** Humans + mascots interact *through* it (cursor-tracking,
  glass-high-five); outsiders can only bonk on it. Never break the rule that our side is warm
  and logged in, theirs is cold and locked out.
- **Mascot continuity is the make-or-break.** Every AI shot uses the same mascot reference
  (Veo 3.1 first-frame/asset-image) — see PRODUCTION §3.3. If a shot morphs the design,
  re-roll; continuity errors are what make AI films feel cheap.
- **AI never makes a product claim.** Every capability is proven in [REC]; AI shots carry
  only story and emotion. The pane (shot 7/8) literally contains real footage.
- **The mock stays archetypal.** Cloud-bot, pixel-reader, terminal-guy — no logos, no names.
  The dunk lands on the *category*, not a person. (The tagging happens in the caption, not the film.)
- **Dead-stop discipline:** shots 10–11 mute ALL stems. 4 seconds of near-silence mid-groove
  is what makes it a dunk instead of a bullet point.
- **v1 fallback** (if the AI world can't hold continuity): straight-cut version — shot order
  1→2 (boring cloud demo) → 4→5→7→8→9→13→14→15→16→18 with MG cards replacing AI connective
  tissue. Same beats, same claim, ~78s.

| Browser Use demos | ~30–60s screen rec, numbered-elements overlay | Speed + legibility — you see each indexed element get clicked; the action loop reads clean | Highlight ring on every driven element (shot 7/8); no timelapse on real work |
