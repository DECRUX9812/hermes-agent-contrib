# Bot Room launch video — storyboard

**Length:** 78s · **Tempo:** 120 BPM (1 bar = 2s — every cut lands on a bar boundary) · **Formats:** 16:9 primary, 9:16 reframe
**One claim:** *Every agent demo dies at the login wall. Bot Room was born behind it.*

Not a feature recital. The film proves one thing three ways: the page comes alive (hook),
it works *as you* (dunk), and any harness can live here (montage).

Shot types: **[REC]** screen recording of the real extension · **[AI]** AI-generated B-roll · **[MG]** motion-graphic card.

## Reference research summary (what we're stealing)

| Clip | Runtime/format | What made it work | What we take |
|---|---|---|---|
| opencode "hides elements" clip | ~30s vertical screen rec, no narration | The *page itself* visibly mutates on a real site — zero explanation needed. Viewer instantly gets "the agent owns the DOM." | Visible DOM mutation as spectacle — our mascots *are* the mutation, plus `dom_hide`/`dom_style` beats. |
| T3 Code launch content (Theo) | X clips + stream segments, desktop app footage | "All your harnesses, one surface" lands instantly; provider-switching mid-thread is the money shot. | Harness-name montage — but our surface is the page, not another app. |
| Browser Use demos | ~30–60s screen rec, numbered-elements overlay | Speed + legibility: you *see* each indexed element get clicked. Action loop is readable. | Keep bot actions legible — highlight ring on the element being driven. |
| OpenAI Operator launch (Jan 2025) | Livestream, cloud remote browser | First big-lab "agent uses the web" moment; human-handoff ("take control") UX. | The inversion: their agent needs *you* to take control at the login wall — ours never asks. |
| Claude computer use (Oct 2024) | ~4min narrated screen rec | Honest messiness on a real desktop; ~5s/action reading made it feel real. | We keep real timing visible — no fake speed. Real logged-in GitHub, real clicks. |
| Project Mariner (Dec 2024) | ~3min narrated demo, Chrome extension | Right instinct — agent in *your* browser, active tab, human-in-the-loop. | The foil: still one silent agent you babysit. We answer with three mascots passing notes. |

## Editorial rules

- **The CAPTCHA beat (shot 6) is the whole pitch.** Everything before it is setup; protect its silence — the dead-stop buzzer against the groove is what makes it a dunk, not a bullet point.
- **Every driven action gets a visible ring** on the element before it fires. Legibility beats speed (the Browser Use lesson); we are deliberately *not* timelapsing the real work.
- **No narrator, no voiceover.** Text callouts carry the argument; the score carries the cut. Max 9 words per callout, sentence case, amber-on-dark chips (WCAG AA — learned the hard way).
- **Real session or nothing.** Shot 8 must show the real user's avatar/notifications and the comment landing under their name — that's the entire claim. Mock the bot *replies* if needed (demo-kit `demo_mock.py` pattern), never the page session.
- **AI-gen footage is garnish, never proof.** B-roll covers dramatization (shots 3, 6, 16) — every capability claim happens in recorded footage.
- **Whip transitions between acts**, freeze-frame + `liveMark` ring to introduce each new bot — a 40px mascot is unreadable at full frame; punch before you stamp.
- **Ending goes for the dunk, not the recap.** The montage names names (Hermes/Grok/OpenClaw/CLI); the lockup repeats the one claim.
